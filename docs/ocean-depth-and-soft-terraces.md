# Ocean depth and soft terrain edges

The ocean now has distinct pale-aqua, bright turquoise, deeper turquoise, teal and navy zones, with soft boundaries. Visual shelf width is derived from nearest-coast distance, nearby inland height, shelter and broad geographic variation. Low beaches receive broader shelves, steep shores narrow shelves, nearby islands share shallow channels, and remote open water stays navy. This bathymetry is rendering data only: it does not alter editable seabed heights, fishing populations, saves or navigation.

Fixed seabed patterns add subdued reef patches and elongated sandbar highlights. Caustics and seabed contour shading are much quieter. Offshore flecks are reduced substantially and directional ripple contrast is subtle. Shoreline highlights are narrow and uneven, foam occurs softly in patches, and rock foam collars are dimmer. Pools retain their clear-water treatment and remain excluded from ocean breakers.

Land and submerged shelf colours move from cream and warm sand through fresh greens and richer upper greens. Each existing elevation level still has a separate cap and a subtly shaded side. Contour corners are cut locally by at most 0.055 world units; the edge bevel is 0.08 wide, 0.035 high and uses three segments. The small rounding stays inside the existing walking-clearance margin. No smoothing is applied to the saved scalar field, no layer thresholds or heights change, and the twenty sculptable terraces remain authoritative.

Validation: visual shelves widen at beaches and narrow at cliffs; shared channels remain shallow and distant ocean deep; shelf data updates only on terrain edits and clears when land is removed; contour rounding stays bounded; geometry is finite and all twenty levels survive; rebuilds preserve every editable height. Terrain metadata and one-layer sculpting/climbing checks, TypeScript, focused lint, local route and production build are included. Browser visual and device-performance testing were not requested.
